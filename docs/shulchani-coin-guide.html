The Coin Guide

Everything you earn is counted in Prutot. These are the seven coins it gets counted into — what each one is worth, and how to read your own balance off the board.

Seven coins

Biggest first. The right-hand column is the one worth memorising: it says how many of the coin below each coin is worth.

Reading a balance

Three things that trip everyone up the first week.

  • Fewest coins winsYour balance is always written with the biggest coins that fit, then the next biggest, down to the Prutah — the way you would hand back change in quarters and dimes rather than in pennies.
  • Earning can shrink your pileSeven Prutah plus one more Prutah is eight Prutah — which is exactly one Isar. You gained, and your balance went from seven coins to one. Fewer coins, more value.
  • Nothing is lost in the changeThe coins are only a way of writing the number down. Behind every balance is a plain count of Prutot, and it never rounds.

How a balance gets broken down

Take the biggest coin that fits, subtract it, and keep going until nothing is left. The bar shows how much of the total each coin is actually carrying.

The pocket card

The same seven coins at card size, to live in a sefer or taped inside a desk. Ten to a sheet either way — pick the label stock you have, or print on plain paper and cut along the guides.
